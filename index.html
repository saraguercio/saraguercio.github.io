HECATE

HECATE: Helium-3 Capture and Transport Expedition

Mission study presented at the 77th International Astronautical Congress (IAC 2026), Antalya, Türkiye

Abstract

HECATE is a feasibility study for a cislunar logistics chain that returns at least 1 kg of lunar Helium-3 to Earth per trip, over four Earth–Moon cycles. The selected “Space Taxi” architecture pairs a reusable, electrically propelled Tug in a 9:2 Near Rectilinear Halo Orbit with expendable Lander, Ascender and Transporter modules. As EDL lead I designed the Sample Return Vehicle that brings the He-3 tank through atmospheric entry at 11 km/s. A 2,000-run Monte Carlo campaign on entry-state, atmospheric-density and aerodynamic dispersions set the design loads. A trade-off on the half-cone angle led to a 45° blunt body with a low ballistic coefficient, and the 95th-percentile heat load sized a PICA heat shield and a cork backshell. A drogue, reefed-main and ringsail main parachute sequence brings the capsule down to a soft landing. I also sized the payload: a composite overwrapped pressure vessel storing 1.2 kg of He-3 at 34.5 MPa, using the Van der Waals equation of state and thin-walled pressure vessel theory.

SUBORB

Manned Suborbital Transport System Design

Abstract

Conceptual design of a human-rated suborbital system that carries a six-person crew and their equipment on an antipodal ballistic trajectory, from Rocket Lab Launch Complex 1 in New Zealand to the Iberian Peninsula. The vehicle is a two-stage expendable launcher derived from Atlas V, with a semi-cryogenic first stage and a cryogenic upper stage, topped by a conical capsule with a tractor-tower launch escape system. I was responsible for the trajectory and the payload definition. The powered ascent was optimized with a direct-shooting method on the discretized steering angle, then coupled through event-based integration with the ballistic coast and a re-entry phase whose entry state was optimized to minimize crew deceleration. The capsule lands about 0.6 km from the target, well inside the 20 km requirement, and both spent stages fall into the ocean. The analysis also showed that a purely ballistic re-entry peaks at 7.45 g, above the 5 g crew limit, so the next design iteration needs a lifting re-entry.

VENTURE

RocketPad: Recovery-as-a-Service Ground Platform

Selected among the top 100 teams for TEF Ignition 3

Abstract

Reusable rockets pay a payload penalty to land: reaching near-zero touchdown velocity takes extra propellant and heavy landing gear, often around 10% of payload capacity. RocketPad moves that burden to the ground with an adaptive landing platform that absorbs high-impact kinetic energy, so vehicles can land faster with less propellant and turn the savings into payload mass. The business plan, developed in the High-Tech Startup course, combines PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, competitor and SWOT analyses. Strategic alternatives were ranked through a multi-criteria AHP analysis, which led to a Recovery-as-a-Service model instead of hardware sales. The value proposition was tested with industry experts, including the MaiaSpace engineering team, who expressed interest in the solution. The team was then selected among the top 100 for TEF Ignition 3, a 7-week venture-building program by Tech Europe Foundation and PoliHub, where we pitched the venture and backed it with technical feasibility studies.

ORBMEC

Interplanetary Explorer & Planetary Explorer Mission

Abstract

The first part designs a transfer from Earth to asteroid N.136789 with a powered gravity assist at Mars, modeled as two Lambert arcs patched at the flyby. A coarse-to-fine grid search over departure, flyby and arrival dates, across a 2030–2060 window sampled on the bodies' synodic periods, minimizes the total Δv. The optimum departs in September 2033 and arrives in July 2036, and the flyby is almost entirely unpowered. The second part studies a highly elliptical Earth orbit (e ≈ 0.41, i = 75°) under J₂ and lunar third-body perturbations. It compares Cartesian (Cowell) and Gauss propagation for accuracy and computational cost, designs a repeating ground track, and validates the models against real TLE data for a LEO debris object and a GEO object.

ADCS

3U CubeSat Attitude Dynamics & Control

Abstract

End-to-end attitude simulation of a 3U CubeSat in low Earth orbit with a 2° nadir-pointing requirement. The model includes gravity-gradient, solar radiation pressure, magnetic and aerodynamic disturbance torques, and a Sun sensor, magnetometer and gyroscope with realistic noise. Attitude is determined by solving Wahba's problem with Davenport's q-method, falling back to the magnetometer alone during eclipse. Actuation combines magnetorquers with four reaction wheels in a pyramid configuration. Three control modes cover the mission: B-dot detumbling, verified through a Monte Carlo campaign with random initial attitudes and rates up to 10 deg/s; a geometric PD slew that recovers a 180° misalignment and acquires the LVLH frame with a residual error well below 1°; and an LQR controller with a state observer that holds nadir pointing within the requirement while staying inside actuator limits.

PROP

MEMS Micro-Thruster Design for a 3U CubeSat

Abstract

Three MEMS micro-propulsion systems (a solid-propellant array, a cold gas thruster and a vaporizing liquid micro-thruster) were designed for a 3U CubeSat against the same requirements of 5 mN thrust and 0.5 m/s Δv, then compared on performance, size and robustness. I worked on the water-fed vaporizing liquid micro-thruster (VLM), using a quasi-one-dimensional model with empirical corrections for micro-scale effects. With the chamber pressure fixed, chamber temperature and expansion ratio were traded to minimize electrical power. Viscous nozzle losses were captured through a Reynolds-dependent discharge coefficient, and the nozzle, heating chamber, injector, feed line and tank were sized. The nominal VLM delivers 5 mN at about 122 s of specific impulse with 1.7 g of water, but needs about 12.7 W, a demanding share of a 3U power budget. Monte Carlo analysis on microfabrication tolerances identified the injector diameter as the most critical dimension.
